CAZATORMENTAS 3D
STORM FORMATION, SUPERCELL STRUCTURE, AND TORNADO IMPLEMENTATION GUIDE

PURPOSE

This document guides future work on the complete storm lifecycle, from
initiation through tornado dynamics. It is written for the current developer,
future Codex sessions, and other AI coding agents.

The goal is not to copy Helicity. Use its readable gameplay presentation as a
reference while building a scientifically grounded system for Uruguay and the
South American Pampas.


NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

- Keep the natural-event model. Atmospheric conditions evolve first; storms
  emerge only when conditions and seeded randomness support them.
- Keep one authoritative active storm.
- Keep authoritative weather rules in app/game/simulation.ts.
- Keep Three.js rendering in app/components/StormScene.tsx.
- Keep radar rendering in app/components/RadarPanel.tsx.
- Keep shared contracts in app/game/types.ts.
- Keep timing, probabilities, dimensions, and weights in app/game/config.ts.
- Keep player-facing UI in Spanish.
- Keep seeded simulation deterministic and randomness injectable for tests.
- Funnel width must not determine EF rating or maximum wind by itself.
- Do not hard-code Northern Hemisphere tornado behavior.
- Do not add hail, lightning, vehicles, probes, damage, multiplayer, or extra
  tornado morphologies unless explicitly requested.
- Do not copy Helicity names, map locations, schedules, or economy.
- Preserve free camera, radar, HUD, simulation pacing, and one-storm behavior.


CURRENT VISUAL BASELINE

- A procedural cloud deck can cover the map as cloud cover increases.
- One randomized procedural supercell volume forms at the simulated storm.
- The supercell changes width, height, depth, lean, and surface billows per
  cycle but remains stable during that cycle.
- The tornado is a ray-marched volume using the same storm palette and related
  procedural density approach as the cloud.
- The tornado extends into the supercell and dissolves inside it to hide the
  connection seam from ground, side, and overhead views.
- Rain, a localized rain curtain, condensation wisps, and ground dust exist.

Treat this as working infrastructure. Extend it instead of replacing it unless
visual evidence shows that its architecture is the source of a defect.


TARGET PLAYER EXPERIENCE

1. CALM PHASE

- Mostly open sky.
- Atmospheric values evolve naturally.
- Radar shows no organized storm.
- No player control directly forces storm formation.

2. CUMULOGENESIS

- Map-wide clouds begin filling the sky.
- A localized rising tower appears at the future supercell position.
- Radar shows a small, weak reflectivity return.
- Updraft growth depends mainly on moisture, instability, and lift.

3. ORGANIZING STORM

- The localized tower expands into a broad, connected storm base.
- The cloud deck darkens and becomes more continuous.
- The forward-flank downdraft precipitation region begins downwind of the
  updraft.
- Radar reflectivity grows in area and intensity.
- Rain placement starts affecting which approach offers a clear view.

4. MATURE SUPERCELL

- A mesocyclone becomes organized inside the main cloud volume.
- A lowered wall-cloud region develops beneath the mesocyclone.
- An inflow sector remains relatively clear.
- A tail cloud may extend from the inflow sector toward the wall cloud.
- A rear-flank downdraft cuts into the rear side of the storm.
- Reflectivity develops an inflow notch and, when rotation is strong enough, a
  hook-like appendage.
- Velocity radar shows an adjacent inbound/outbound couplet.

5. TORNADOGENESIS

- A funnel develops from the center of the integrated wall-cloud lowering.
- The wall cloud must be part of the main procedural cloud density. Do not add
  a visibly separate fake cloud-base mesh.
- Surface circulation may begin before full condensation reaches the ground.
- The tornado center follows the mesocyclone with limited natural meander.
- RFD wrapping and FFD placement determine whether the tornado is rain-wrapped
  or clearly visible.

6. DISSIPATION

- Tornado intensity and condensation weaken naturally.
- Surface circulation and debris fade.
- Wall-cloud rotation loses organization.
- Velocity couplet and hook signature weaken.
- Local supercell fades before the map-wide cloud deck fully clears.


STORM INITIATION AND MOTION

- Add a simulated time-of-day only if it fits the existing cycle model cleanly.
- Favor daytime and evening initiation, but do not copy Helicity's exact hours
  without an explicit design decision.
- Spawn a storm near the map edge opposite its motion vector so it crosses the
  playable area.
- Typical Pampas storm motion may favor a southwest-to-northeast track, but
  other motion vectors must remain possible.
- Derive storm-relative structure from the motion vector. Never assume that
  north, south, east, or west always represents a specific flank.
- Shear should influence storm speed, updraft tilt, organization, and motion.
- CAPE should influence updraft strength and potential storm intensity.
- Moisture should influence cloud depth, precipitation, and condensation.


AUTHORITATIVE SUPERCELL STRUCTURE

The simulation should expose reusable, language-neutral structure values.
Exact contracts may evolve, but they should represent:

- stormMotion: horizontal motion vector and speed.
- structureOrientation: storm-relative orientation in world space.
- updraftPosition: main rising-air center.
- mesocyclonePosition: rotating updraft center.
- mesocycloneStrength: normalized organization/rotation.
- rotationDirection: clockwise or counterclockwise.
- wallCloudStrength: lowering and organization below the mesocyclone.
- inflowStrength: air feeding the updraft.
- inflowDirection: storm-relative clear-sector direction.
- ffdIntensity and ffdPosition: forward-flank precipitation.
- rfdIntensity and rfdPosition: rear-flank descending-air sector.
- rfdCutStrength: amount of cloud/rain cleared behind the mesocyclone.
- tailCloudStrength and tailCloudDirection.
- hookStrength: radar hook organization.

All values must derive from atmospheric state, lifecycle progress, storm
profile, and deterministic randomness. Rendering must consume them without
creating authoritative meteorology.


VISUAL SUPERCELL STRUCTURE

GLOBAL CLOUD DECK

- Cover the map progressively during formation.
- Begin patchy, become solid during maturity, then break during dissipation.
- Keep moving underside texture and storm-dependent darkness.
- Hide geometry edges at the horizon.

LOCAL SUPERCELL

- Remain one connected procedural volume.
- Grow from a tower into a broad, rounded storm body.
- Connect visually into the global deck.
- Keep cycle-to-cycle variation within believable bounds.
- Avoid holes, detached lobes, perfect disks, stacked rings, and hard caps.

WALL CLOUD

- Implement as a density lowering inside the same supercell shader.
- Center it beneath the simulated mesocyclone.
- Increase lowering and rotation with wallCloudStrength.
- Use its calculated underside as the tornado attachment height.

TAIL CLOUD

- Implement as a connected density extension toward the inflow sector.
- Keep it optional and strength-dependent.
- It should visually lead toward the wall cloud, not become a detached tube.

RFD CUT AND HORSESHOE

- Subtract or erode cloud density behind the mesocyclone.
- Keep the cut asymmetric and tied to RFD direction and strength.
- A horseshoe-shaped opening may emerge at strong organization.
- Do not guarantee this feature for every storm.

PRECIPITATION

- Move broad precipitation into the FFD instead of centering all rain on the
  tornado.
- Keep a clearer inflow notch between FFD and the rotating updraft.
- Add finer wrapping precipitation in the RFD only when structure supports it.
- Let motion direction determine whether the tornado becomes rain-wrapped.


RADAR MODEL

REFLECTIVITY

- Begin as a small weak blob during initiation.
- Expand and intensify with FFD precipitation.
- Place the strongest broad return in the FFD.
- Form an inflow notch beside the mesocyclone.
- Wrap a narrower RFD return around the circulation as hookStrength grows.
- Derive the display from simulation structure, not independent decoration.
- Do not add a hail core until hail is explicitly requested.

VELOCITY

- Calculate radial velocity relative to the radar site.
- Project local wind vectors onto the radar-to-sample direction.
- Show nearby inbound and outbound values around the mesocyclone.
- Couplets must rotate correctly when rotationDirection changes.
- RFD winds must remain distinguishable from the tornadic couplet.
- Do not hard-code red or green to a world-space side.


TORNADO FORMATION AND CONNECTION

- Tornado formation requires sufficient mesocyclone organization, wall-cloud
  lowering, low-level circulation, and weighted randomness.
- Surface circulation may precede a fully condensed funnel.
- The visible tornado top must meet the calculated wall-cloud underside.
- Extend tornado density a short distance inside the supercell and dissolve it
  before its volume boundary. This hides seams from all camera angles.
- Use the same storm-dependent color family and compatible density noise as the
  supercell.
- Keep a darker internal core and soft turbulent edge.
- Keep condensation wisps subtle; avoid obvious bright dots.
- Keep debris and dust near the ground and tied to simulated availability.


TORNADO WIND FIELD

Do not treat the tornado as a uniform circular wind value.

At any world position, calculate:

ground wind =
  rotational wind
  + radial inflow
  + tornado translation
  + local RFD contribution

Required behavior:

- A relative-lull core may exist, especially in wider tornadoes.
- Peak winds occur in a ring outside the core, often inside the visible funnel.
- Winds weaken outside the main circulation.
- Translation adds to rotation on one side and subtracts on the other.
- The additive side has stronger ground-relative winds and may extend farther.
- The subtractive side has weaker winds closer to the core.
- Peak-wind radius should vary independently from visible funnel width.
- Small tornadoes may not have an obvious calm center.

SOUTHERN HEMISPHERE RULE

- Counterclockwise rotation usually produces an additive right side relative
  to forward motion.
- Clockwise rotation usually produces an additive left side.
- Uruguay gameplay must calculate this from rotation direction and motion.
- Never label the right side as universally strongest.

Future probes, vehicles, damage, and intercept scoring should consume this wind
field. Do not implement those systems as part of the storm-structure work
unless explicitly requested.


RECOMMENDED IMPLEMENTATION ORDER

1. Add structure contracts to app/game/types.ts.
2. Add timing, motion, and structure ranges to app/game/config.ts.
3. Generate deterministic storm motion and structural profile parameters.
4. Evolve structure values through lifecycle stages in simulation.ts.
5. Add tests for deterministic structure, valid ranges, motion orientation,
   rotation direction, and lifecycle timing.
6. Update RadarPanel.tsx reflectivity from FFD/RFD/inflow structure.
7. Add velocity radar derived from world wind vectors.
8. Feed structure values into StormScene.tsx.
9. Integrate wall-cloud lowering into main cloud density.
10. Add tail-cloud density and RFD erosion.
11. Reposition precipitation into FFD and RFD regions.
12. Attach tornado to integrated wall-cloud underside.
13. Implement reusable tornado wind-field sampling.
14. Add Spanish HUD explanations only after underlying behavior is stable.
15. Perform live visual review from ground, side, overhead, inflow, FFD, and
    rear-flank viewpoints.


TEST REQUIREMENTS

Add deterministic tests covering:

- Same seed produces identical storm motion and structure.
- Different cycles produce bounded variation.
- Storm enters from a map edge appropriate to its motion.
- FFD stays on the correct storm-relative flank.
- RFD and inflow notch occupy different sectors.
- Mesocyclone, wall cloud, and tornado remain spatially connected.
- Hook strength grows only after sufficient organization.
- Velocity couplet reverses correctly with rotation direction.
- Tornado additive side changes with rotation direction and motion.
- EF distribution remains weighted toward EF1-EF3.
- Funnel width remains a poor predictor of EF strength.
- Tornado begins weak, intensifies, and weakens.

Required validation for behavior or architecture changes:

npm run lint
npm test

Also run npm run build for dependency, routing, deployment, or build changes.
Run git diff --check before handoff.


VISUAL REVIEW CHECKLIST

- Cloud deck covers the map without visible borders.
- Supercell reads as one connected storm.
- Base has no accidental holes, spikes, stacked rings, or flat box cuts.
- Wall cloud looks integrated, not pasted underneath.
- Tail cloud points toward wall cloud and remains attached.
- FFD obscures the expected approach sector.
- Inflow sector offers a clearer tornado view.
- RFD cut is visible only when organization supports it.
- Tornado meets cloud correctly from ground, side, and overhead.
- Tornado color and density match the supercell.
- Tornado remains much smaller than the full supercell.
- Radar structure agrees with the visible storm.
- Frame rate remains acceptable after every shader change.

DEFINITION OF DONE

The storm system is complete when:

- Formation follows atmospheric evolution.
- Global deck and local supercell remain connected.
- Mesocyclone, FFD, RFD, inflow, wall cloud, tail cloud, and hook are coherent.
- Radar and rendering consume the same authoritative structure.
- Tornado forms beneath the mesocyclone from the integrated cloud lowering.
- Tornado wind asymmetry uses vectors and Southern Hemisphere rotation.
- UI remains Spanish and accessible.
- Existing camera, pacing, radar, and simulation behavior remain usable.
- Tests, lint, build checks, and focused visual review pass.
